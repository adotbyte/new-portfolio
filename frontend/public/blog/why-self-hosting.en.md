# Why I Chose Self-Hosting and What It Gave Me

My first computer arrived at home in 2001, and the internet in 2003. From day one, that "internet" world hooked me so much that I wanted to understand how everything works "under the hood." Even back then, looking at an old desktop computer, I dreamed of turning it into a server. Truth be told, the cost of electricity seemed too intimidating at the time, so the dream was left in the drawer.

But the desire never went away. The moment I heard about the *Raspberry Pi*, I realized — here was my chance. It seemed like the perfect project: small, quiet, energy-efficient. Still, it wasn't until February 2025 that I finally understood I truly needed one. And not just any version, but right away the 16GB RAM version with NVMe support. And so a "raspberry" with two 2TB NVMe drives appeared in my home.

Thus began my personal journey into **self-hosting**.

## Where Did I Start?

My main goal was to learn how to use **Docker** and **Cloudflare Tunnels**. I wanted all my services to be isolated, easily updatable, and securely accessible from the internet without opening unnecessary ports. It was like a new game, where every step opened up more and more possibilities.

But then something happened that I completely didn't expect. With the arrival of AI tools (like Claude), I began not only using other people's applications, but also **building my own**. That was yet another dream — programming. Before, it seemed too hard, because I imagined that every error would take hours upon hours to debug through Google searches.

## What Did It Give Me?

Today, looking back, I see that self-hosting gave me far more than just a working server:

1. **An understanding of how the internet works.** From DNS, SSL to reverse proxy and Cloudflare Zero Trust. Now I'm no longer just a "user," but a person who understands what happens behind every click.
2. **The courage to experiment.** When you know you can restore everything (if you have backups), you're no longer afraid to break anything. This lets you learn from your mistakes.
3. **A new perspective on AI.** I realized that AI isn't just an "answer machine." It's a tool that can help you cross those boundaries that previously seemed insurmountable. Today I write code that a year ago I would have considered impossible.
4. **Confidence in myself.** Although my path started with "I want to see how Docker works," today I have automated backups, monitoring, and my own AI agent that lives in my portfolio.

## What Do I Want to Tell Others?

If you're hesitating about whether it's worth starting, or whether it's "too hard" for you — know that I thought the same way too. The most important thing is to start with a small step. Bought a Raspberry Pi? Great. Install Docker. Launch one container. Then a second. And so, little by little.

Today my home lab runs 24/7. It maintains itself, makes backups, and even sends me notifications if something goes wrong. It's not magic. It's simply a path walked step by step.

And believe me — that path is worth every hour.

# Why I Chose Self-Hosting and What It Gave Me

My first computer arrived at home in 2001, and the internet came in 2003. From day one, that "internet" world hooked me so much that I wanted to understand how everything works "under the hood." Even back then, looking at an old desktop computer, I dreamed of turning it into a server. To be fair, the cost of electricity seemed too daunting at the time, so the dream stayed in the drawer.

But the desire never went away. The moment I heard about the **Raspberry Pi**, I knew — here was my chance. It seemed like the perfect project: small, quiet, energy-efficient. Even so, it wasn't until February 2025 that I finally realized I truly needed one. And not just any version, but right away the 16GB RAM model with NVMe support. That's how a "raspberry" with two 2TB NVMe drives ended up in my home.

So began my personal journey into **self-hosting**.

## Where Did I Start?

My main goal was to learn how to use **Docker** and **Cloudflare Tunnels**. I wanted all my services to be isolated, easy to update, and securely accessible from the internet without opening unnecessary ports. It was like a new game, where every step opened up more and more possibilities.

But then something happened that I didn't expect at all. With the arrival of AI tools (like Claude), I began not only using other people's applications but also **building my own**. That was another dream — programming. Before, it seemed too hard, because I imagined I'd have to debug every error for hours through Google searches.

## What Did It Give Me?

Today, looking back, I see that self-hosting gave me far more than just a working server:

1. **An understanding of how the internet works.** From DNS and SSL to reverse proxies and Cloudflare Zero Trust. Now I'm no longer just a "user," but someone who understands what's happening behind every click.
2. **The courage to experiment.** When you know you can restore everything (if you have backups), you're no longer afraid of breaking anything. This lets you learn from your mistakes.
3. **A new perspective on AI.** I realized that AI isn't just an "answer machine." It's a tool that can help you push past the limits that once seemed insurmountable. Today I write code that a year ago I would have considered impossible.
4. **Self-confidence.** Although my journey started with "I want to see how Docker works," today I have automated backups, monitoring, and my own AI agent that lives in my portfolio.

## What Do I Want to Tell Others?

If you're hesitating about whether it's worth starting, or whether it's "too hard" for you — know that I thought the same. The most important thing is to start with a small step. Bought a Raspberry Pi? Great. Install Docker. Launch one container. Then a second. And so, little by little.

Today my home lab runs 24/7. It maintains itself, makes backups, and even sends me notifications if something goes wrong. It's not magic. It's simply a path walked step by step.

And trust me — that path is worth every hour.
